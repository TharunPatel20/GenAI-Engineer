ACME ROBOTICS - INTERNAL NOTES (FICTIONAL)
This document is invented for a classroom exercise. Acme Robotics is not a real company.

COMPANY OVERVIEW
Acme Robotics is a fictional maker of warehouse and factory robots.
It is headquartered in Springfield Valley and was founded in 2014.
It has about 850 employees.

PRODUCT LINES
1. ShelfMover: an autonomous pallet robot for warehouses. Launched in 2018.
2. PickArm X2: a robotic arm that sorts small parts. Launched in 2022.
3. InspectBot: a wheeled robot that checks factory floors with cameras. Launched in 2024.
4. Acme Cloud: a subscription dashboard that monitors every Acme robot. Launched in 2023.

2025 FINANCIAL SUMMARY (USD)
- Total revenue: 62.4 million, up 11 percent from 56.2 million in 2024.
- Hardware revenue: 38.1 million.
- Subscription revenue: 24.3 million, up 27 percent.
- Operating loss: 3.2 million, improved from 5.8 million in 2024.
- Cash on hand at year end: 19.7 million.
- Active business customers: 214, up from 176.
- Customer retention rate: 91 percent.

2025 HIGHLIGHTS
- Subscription revenue grew fastest. It is now 39 percent of total revenue.
- The PickArm X2 was the best-selling product by units.
- The main risk was late component deliveries in the first half of 2025.

2026 AI INVESTMENT PLAN
Acme Robotics plans to invest 9 million dollars in artificial intelligence during 2026.
The plan has three parts:
1. Vision models: 4 million dollars to train better object recognition for ShelfMover and InspectBot.
2. Planning software: 3 million dollars to build a new route planner. The target is a 15 percent cut in travel time inside warehouses.
3. Internal AI tools: 2 million dollars for document search and reporting tools used by staff.

HIRING
- 25 new engineers will be hired in 2026.
- 12 of the 25 will work on machine learning.

MILESTONES
- Q2 2026: release the new route planner in beta to 10 pilot customers.
- Q4 2026: decide whether to open a second research office.

RISKS
- Vision models may lose accuracy in dusty or low-light factories.
- Larger robotics firms may compete on price.
