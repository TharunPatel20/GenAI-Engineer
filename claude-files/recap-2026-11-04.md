### 4. Parts of a Chain
- Each chain has three components:
  1. **Prompt:** guides the chain. Each chain needs its own prompt.
  2. **LLM:** the model used to run the chain.
  3. **Output parser:** the Structured Output Parser. It removes extra metadata from the model's response so the output is cleaner. The instructor said this would be covered in more depth later.
- **Prompt Template** (`PromptTemplate`):
  - Creates reusable prompts with input variables.
  - Connects chains together. Chain 1's output is passed to Chain 2 through the template's input variable.

### 5. Hallucination and How to Reduce Its Impact
- **Hallucination:** an LLM confidently gives wrong or misleading information. The instructor said LLMs can answer generic questions well, but they sometimes confuse or misguide users.
- Three ways to reduce the impact, as stated:
  1. **Prompt engineering:** make the expectation and output structure clear.
  2. **RAG (Retrieval-Augmented Generation):** give the model relevant retrieved documents before it answers.
  3. **Fine-tuning:** further training of the model on specific data.
- **Comparison (cost and complexity):**

| Approach | Cost | Complexity |
|---|---|---|
| Prompt engineering | Least expensive | Least complex (close to plain English) |
| RAG | Moderate | Moderately complex |
| Fine-tuning | Most expensive (many GPUs, large power use) | Most complex |

- The instructor said fine-tuning is popular but not widely used at enterprise scale because of cost and complexity.

### 6. RAG Pipeline (Introduction)
- **Why RAG:** the document is too large to pass to the LLM at once.
- **Step 1, Data ingestion:** load the source document (for example a PDF, web page, or Excel sheet).
- **Step 2, Chunking:** split the document into smaller pieces (chunks). Neighboring chunks overlap by some tokens to keep context consistent.
- **Step 3, Embeddings:** convert each chunk from text into a numeric vector. The instructor named techniques such as N-grams, Bag of Words, and TF-IDF (Term Frequency–Inverse Document Frequency). He said he would cover one of them in the next session.
- **Step 4, Vector store:** store the embeddings in a **vector database** (also called a vector store). This session did not name a specific product.
- **Step 5, Query embedding:** convert the user's question into an embedding with the same method.
- **Step 6, Similarity search:** compare the query embedding with the stored embeddings. Measures named: Euclidean distance, Manhattan distance, and cosine similarity. These are mathematical measures. An LLM is not needed for the embedding step.
- **Step 7, Retrieval:** return the most relevant stored content.
- **Step 8, Augmentation and generation:** the LLM uses the retrieved content to produce a user-friendly answer.
- Participant question (Renju Nair): does embedding need an LLM? The instructor said no. The same method must be used for both the stored chunks and the query.
- Analogy: compare like with like, the way you compare kilograms with kilograms.
