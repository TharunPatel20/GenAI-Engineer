### 4. Transformer Basics (Introduction)
- The Transformer is shown as a black box with two parts: an **encoder** on the left and a **decoder** on the right. The instructor drew six stacked blocks on each side.
- **Encoder:** takes the whole input at once.
- **Decoder:** generates the output one token at a time (Ŷ1, then Ŷ2, then Ŷ3).
- Input text is converted to numbers through embeddings (for example, word embeddings or bag-of-words). The instructor gave example numbers only, such as 11, 16, 32, and 12.
- The numeric representation is called a **context vector**. It is passed into the decoder so the decoder knows the input.
- Example: an email auto-complete for "Please find the report". The four words are X1 to X4 and the model predicts the next word. Each new prediction is fed back as input for the next step, so the output grows one word at a time.
- Uses named: next-word prediction, language translation, sentiment analysis, and named entity recognition.

### 7. Prompting Strategies
- **Zero-shot prompting:** a single instruction with no examples. Best for simple, straightforward tasks.
  - Example: classify a hotel customer feedback as positive, negative, or neutral. A one-line prompt was enough.
  - The instructor said a language model like an LLM already understands simple text well.
- **Few-shot prompting:** the instruction is given along with examples or references.
  - Example: an HR department classifying resumes for a machine learning engineer role as "strong fit", "potential fit", or "low fit". These terms are subjective, so examples define them.
- **Chain-of-thought prompting:** the model is asked to reason through steps.
  - Example: a bank loan decision with a multi-step check. For example, income of at least $50,000 and credit score of at least 700, plus other checks. The instructor said this kind of reasoning needs the model to think step by step, not just answer.
- **Choosing a strategy:** use zero-shot for simple tasks, few-shot when the task is subjective and needs examples, and chain-of-thought when reasoning over multiple conditions is needed.
